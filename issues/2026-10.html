This month in brief

Pick of the month

Tyrosine kinase inhibitors in metastatic pheochromocytomas and paragangliomas: an updated systematic review and meta-analysis

Xu X et al. · Endocr Relat Cancer · PPGL Practice-informing

FindingsSystematic review and meta-analysis, 11 studies (7 interventional, 4 observational), n=246. Pooled objective response rate 27.7% (95% CI 21.6–34.3%) and disease control rate 84.2% (74.8–91.9%); median PFS 13.4 months (7.9–14.7), median OS 26.7 months (24.9–49.4).

Stable disease was the most frequent best response (51.8%), and 11.3% of patients stopped treatment for adverse events. Responses were numerically higher in SDHB-related disease (36.8% vs 20.1%, p=0.06). Sunitinib did worse than the other TKIs pooled together, with response rates of 21.2% vs 36.9% (p=0.04) and disease control of 72.6% vs 94.5% (p=0.001). Limits: mix of observational and interventional studies, heterogeneous populations and treatment lines, indirect comparisons between agents.

In Italy: sunitinib and cabozantinib are available but not licensed for PPGL; their use in this setting is off-label.

Take-homeTKIs control disease in most patients with metastatic PPGL, but objective responses remain a minority. The weaker results with sunitinib come from indirect comparisons and need confirmation before they guide the choice of agent.

Pheochromocytoma and paraganglioma

Clinical and systemic therapies

Efficacy and safety of sunitinib in treatment of advanced or metastatic pheochromocytomas and paragangliomas: A meta-analysis of sunitinib-treated arms from prospective clinical trials

Román-González A et al. · J Clin Endocrinol Metab Practice-informing

FindingsMeta-analysis of the sunitinib arms of 3 prospective trials, n=114. Objective response rate 21.2% (95% CI 11.7–35.3%), all partial responses; median PFS 11.9 months (6.9–16.9), 12-month PFS 48.2%.

Stable disease was the best response in 51.8% and progression in 27.1%. Grade 3 or higher adverse events occurred in 57.8%, with high heterogeneity across trials (I² 86%), and 13.2% discontinued for toxicity. Only one trial had a comparator, so the synthesis was done at the level of treatment arms. Limits: three trials only, low to very low certainty of evidence.

In Italy: sunitinib is available but not licensed for PPGL; its use in this setting is off-label.

Take-homeProspective data support sunitinib as a disease-control option in metastatic PPGL, with responses in about one patient in five and frequent grade 3 toxicity. The broader TKI meta-analysis in the Pick of the month finds the same response rate.

Surgery and loco-regional therapies

Baroreflex Failure After Surgical Resection of Bilateral Carotid Body Tumors: A Systematic Review

Graboyes AZ et al. · Laryngoscope Hypothesis-generating

FindingsPRISMA systematic review, 15 case reports and 8 case series, with no cohort studies or trials available. Baroreflex failure usually started right after the second operation and often improved over time.

Clonidine and benzodiazepines were the treatments most often used. Long-term outcomes were reported inconsistently, and the abstract gives no incidence. The authors conclude that the clinical course is more variable and often less severe than commonly perceived. Limits: case-level evidence only, no denominator to estimate risk, uneven follow-up.

Take-homeThe risk of baroreflex failure after bilateral carotid body surgery cannot be quantified from the published literature. The decision to resect the second tumour still rests on age, tumour behaviour, genotype and the patient's goals.

Genetics, surveillance and prognosis

Locally Advanced Disease and Clinical Outcomes of Pheochromocytomas and Paragangliomas: Insights from a Multicenter Study

Icin BB et al. · Endocr Pract Practice-informing

FindingsRetrospective cohort from 19 tertiary centres in Türkiye, n=389, 111 (28.5%) with locally advanced disease. Local invasion independently predicted persistent or recurrent disease (HR 3.64; 95% CI 1.55–8.52; p=0.003), with 5-year DFS 81.8% vs 93.4%.

Locally advanced disease was defined as capsular, periadrenal fat, adjacent-organ or vascular invasion. Persistent or recurrent disease occurred in 17.1% vs 6.1%. The association held after excluding patients with distant metastases at diagnosis and in stage I–II tumours (5-year DFS 84.8% vs 95.5%), where adding local invasion to TNM improved model fit. With both local invasion and Ki-67 ≥3%, 5-year DFS was 68.5%. Limits: retrospective, no central pathology review mentioned, genetic status not reported in the abstract.

Take-homeLocal invasion on the pathology report identifies PPGL patients who need closer follow-up, including those with stage I–II disease. Its combination with Ki-67 is a simple risk tool that deserves validation.

Biological and translational

Effects of a GLP-1 agonist and antidiabetic therapies on pheochromocytoma/paraganglioma growth

Wang K et al. · Endocr Relat Cancer Hypothesis-generating

FindingsTranslational study on 54 fresh patient-derived PPGL primary cultures, correlated with 67 patients from the prospective PROSPHEO cohort. Semaglutide had antitumour effects in cluster 1 PPGL but promoted growth at high dose in 3 tumours with germline RET variants.

The cultures included 8 cluster 1 and 18 cluster 2/3 tumours, 4 of them with RET variants; a tumour with a somatic RET variant did not show the growth-promoting effect. Metformin had an overall antitumour effect, while insulin promoted growth in individual cultures. In the clinical cohort, patients on insulin had larger median tumour size than those on metformin or GLP-1 receptor agonists, or on no antidiabetic therapy. Limits: in vitro endpoints, very small genotype subgroups, cross-sectional clinical correlation.

Take-homeDiabetes is common in PPGL, and these data suggest that the choice of antidiabetic drug might influence tumour growth. Prudence with GLP-1 receptor agonists in MEN2 carriers seems reasonable until clinical data are available.
